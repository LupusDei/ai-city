First Light

We have built an excellent simulation engine, and we have not built a game. 923 tests at ~100% coverage prove the physics are right. Nobody can play it.

The finding

This is not "the UI is rough." There is no entry point, no way to start a game, and no game content outside test fixtures. Every structure roster in this project — habitat, reactor, hopper, press — exists only as a fixture inside a test file. We have the nouns of the game written down nowhere a player could ever reach them.

The engine work was not wasted — it is the foundation, and it is genuinely good. But a simulation nobody can start is not a game.

Three tiers of missing

Tier 1 — Playable

Can a human start it and reach turn 278? Needs a content roster, a new-game flow, a renderer, the three screens, input wiring, save/load, and pacing.

Tier 2 — Legible

Can they understand why they are losing? CycleReport already carries the cut line and the shed rationale. Nothing surfaces it. The power-versus-labour death spiral is the heart of the design and is currently invisible.

Tier 3 — Fun

Do they want to play again? A tuned difficulty curve, an environment that does something, a recovery mechanic so a bad turn 90 is not a silent 180-turn loss, and an ending worth reaching.

The shape of the gap

Comparison with Raynor's work

Raynor has three resource-chain epics planned and correctly dependency-gated — Regolith → Shielded Habitat, Silica → Solar, Ice → Air → Life Support. Roughly 92 beads, well specified, no competing roots, nothing startable before its prerequisites. The planning is sound.

They are the right work in the wrong order.

  • They add depth to a game nobody can play yet. Depth is only felt through play.
  • They would be balanced blind. Tuning a silicon furnace against a loop no human has ever run is guesswork with a test suite attached.
  • His own gating already says this. The chains are the accepted proposal's deferred backlog, conditional on the core loop being fun — and that condition has never been evaluated, because the loop has never been played.

Nothing here is wasted. First Light gives the chains a place to land and a way to measure them. Afterwards, "is the silicon detour worth 18 turns?" is a question we can answer rather than estimate.

The epic

1
Vocabulary. A real production structure roster in src/, not test fixtures. The game's actual nouns, authored as data.
2
Start. Bootstrap landing → colony, and a new-game flow that ends with a colony on screen. Currently in flight as aic-hfb.
3
See. Canvas grid renderer plus the survey, operations and cycle-report screens. Gated on the React toolchain already in progress.
4
Play. Input → typed orders (applyOrders already exists and is tested), fast-forward to next event, and save/load. 278 manual turns is not a session; it is an endurance test.
5
Drama. An event scheduler so dust storms actually occur. The environment is already plumbed and inert — making it live costs little and changes the texture of every game.
6
Tune. A balance pass driven by the headless runner. The death spiral must be reachable and recoverable if caught early — reachable or it is not a game, recoverable or it is a punishment.
7
Land. End-game presentation, and a first-five-turns onboarding that teaches by playing rather than by explaining.

Success criteria

A person who has never seen this can sit down, found a colony, feel the power-versus-labour squeeze, lose, understand exactly why, and immediately want another go.

Measured by an actual playtest, not asserted. Plus the engineering floor that already holds: determinism intact, golden trace passing, gates green.

Recommendation

Build First Light next. Hold the chains at their current gate. Then re-read the accepted proposal's own condition — "do not build these until the core loop is fun" — with evidence in hand instead of intuition.
